Alarm settings for your transmitter

Suggested telemetry alarms, worked out from your own flights and common practice. Each value says why, and how sure it is.

DEMO Based on 1 synthetic flight. With your logs the numbers come from all loaded flights.

Question it answers

“Where should I set the alarms on my radio so they warn in time but don’t go off on every climb?”

Uses

  • mAh per motor climb
  • Voltage sag and internal resistance
  • Peak temperatures, receiver voltage

Rules

  • Never riskier than the common rule (80 %, 3.3 V under load)
  • Fewer than 3 flights: only the common rule

Limits

  • Menu names differ per radio; the NX8 manual lists alarm types Inh and Tone
  • No suggestion for frame losses or holds

Suggestions from your logs and common hobby practice, not manufacturer settings. Change telemetry settings only with the model switched off: the NX8 manual warns that leaving the telemetry menu briefly interrupts the radio signal.

Battery ageing

Internal resistance of each pack over weeks of flying, with a forecast of when it reaches the retire level.

DEMO Invented history of three packs over four months. With your logs every flight of a smart battery adds a point.

Question it answers

“Which pack is wearing out, and how long until I should retire it?”

Uses

  • Resistance per flight (already estimated)
  • Smart battery ID and cycle count
  • Battery temperature

Method

  • Cold flights read high: values below 20 °C are corrected
  • Trend over the last 10 flights
  • Forecast only with ≥ 5 flights over ≥ 2 weeks

Needs

All flights loaded at once, or files kept in the browser (planned storage). Packs without ID cannot be told apart.

Internal resistance per cell

Dots are flights (hollow = cold, corrected). Lines are the trend. Shading: up to 10 mΩ excellent, 12 acceptable, above 15 retire.

The resistance is estimated from the voltage drop under motor load and includes wires and plugs. It is good for comparing a pack with itself over time, not for comparing with a charger’s reading.
